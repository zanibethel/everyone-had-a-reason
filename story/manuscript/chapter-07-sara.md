# Chapter 7
## Sara

Sara had been on the Meridian less than twenty minutes and already wanted everyone to stop talking to her.

That probably wasn’t fair.

Nobody had actually done anything wrong.

Benji and Eli had been sweet.

Adrian had been welcoming.

Theo seemed harmless.

Even Zac had managed to make it from the parking lot to their room without asking her twelve times whether she was okay.

That alone should have counted as a good start.

She dropped her bag onto the bed.

The cabin was nicer than she expected.

Not huge, but clean. Dark wood, soft lighting, one wide window looking out over the marina.

Zac set his bag beside the wall.

“I’m gonna look around.”

Sara glanced at him.

“Okay.”

“You want anything?”

“No.”

He nodded.

“Okay.”

That was it.

No second question.

No *You sure?*

No standing there waiting for her to change her mind.

He left.

Sara looked at the closed door for a moment.

Then exhaled.

Better.

She opened her bag and started unpacking.

Her phone buzzed.

Another message about the fight.

She flipped it facedown.

The whole point of this trip had been to disappear for a little while.

No cameras.

No interviews.

No people asking how it felt to win.

No people asking what was next.

No people deciding what her face meant every time she wasn’t smiling.

She shoved a pair of shorts into the drawer.

The wink flashed through her mind again.

Adrian’s quick smile.

Then the wink at Zac.

And Zac claiming he hadn’t even noticed it.

Maybe it was nothing.

Probably was.

She hated that her brain had bothered keeping it.

Sara bent over her bag again.

“Should’ve just gone somewhere by myself.”

“Rough start?”

Sara straightened.

Elena stood in the doorway with a stack of folded towels against one arm.

“How long have you been standing there?”

Elena frowned.

“I just got here.”

Sara looked toward the hallway.

The door had been left partly open after Zac walked out.

Elena stepped inside and set the towels at the end of the bed.

“Everything okay?”

“I’m fine.”

“Okay.”

Elena turned to leave.

Sara looked back at her bag.

“You gonna tell anybody?”

Elena stopped.

“Tell anybody what?”

“That I said that.”

“No.”

Elena reached for the empty towel basket beside Sara’s open bag.

Her bracelet caught against the strap.

A small metallic snap.

Elena pulled her wrist free.

“What was that?” Sara asked.

“Just caught it.”

Elena turned her wrist over.

Sara noticed the bracelet then—silver, crowded with little charms that shifted against each other when Elena moved.

Elena gave it a quick look, apparently satisfied, then lifted the basket.

Sara watched her.

Elena shrugged.

“It’s none of my business.”

Something about the phrase landed wrong.

Sara looked at her.

“You’re right.”

Her voice sharpened.

“It is none of your business.”

Footsteps passed outside.

Benji and Eli.

Both heard it.

Benji glanced through the doorway at Sara, then at Elena.

Elena stepped into the hall.

Benji gave her a small, questioning thumbs-up.

*You good?*

Elena nodded once.

Benji accepted it.

He and Eli kept walking.

Elena headed the other direction.

Sara stood there for a moment.

The irritation faded almost immediately.

She looked down at the open bag.

Finished unpacking.

Then zipped it shut.

By the time Sara reached the kitchen, the door was closed.

She tried it anyway.

Locked.

A small sign beside it read:

**SERVICE BEGINS AFTER DEPARTURE**

“Of course.”

Her stomach growled.

Sara looked down the corridor.

Too many voices below.

Too many people settling in.

She turned toward the stairs instead.

If she couldn’t eat yet, at least she could be somewhere nobody expected anything from her.

A few minutes later, she stepped onto the upper deck.

Wind moved across the water.

The marina stretched behind them.

Sara found an empty section away from everyone else and sat down alone.
