# Chapter 3
## Sara

By the time Sara saw the Meridian, she was already tired of the trip.

Not the boat.

The boat looked nice.

Small enough that it didn’t feel like a floating hotel. Expensive enough that somebody had clearly spent money making sure every angle looked good in a picture.

It was everything around it that had started wearing on her.

The fight.

The messages after the fight.

People sending clips of the fight.

People asking when the next fight was.

People asking if she thought she could beat somebody she’d never met because they saw fifteen seconds of each of them online.

And now this.

A getaway where she was supposed to relax.

With people.

Sara adjusted the strap of her bag and kept walking.

Beside her, Zac looked toward the boat.

“That’s it.”

“I figured.”

“You excited?”

She looked at him.

He smiled.

“Right. Stupid question.”

“Very.”

“I’m learning.”

“That remains to be seen.”

He laughed softly and kept pace beside her.

The port was quiet compared with the places she had been lately. No crowd. No cameras. No announcer trying to make everything sound more important than it was.

Just water, concrete, gulls, and the occasional clatter of something metal against a mast.

That part she liked.

She wanted two days where nobody needed anything from her.

She wanted to sit somewhere and not be watched.

Maybe sleep.

Maybe read.

Maybe do absolutely nothing and not have somebody ask if something was wrong.

That last part sounded especially good.

A little farther ahead, two guys were walking toward the same dock.

One of them looked back.

Then again.

Sara noticed immediately.

She had gotten good at noticing.

The second one said something under his breath.

The first elbowed him.

Sara sighed through her nose.

Zac looked over.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

The younger-looking one turned again.

This time he stopped pretending.

“Wait.”

Sara kept walking.

He looked directly at her.

“Are you Sara?”

There it was.

She put on the smile.

Not fake exactly.

Just practiced.

“Depends who’s asking.”

His whole face changed.

“No way.”

The other one looked embarrassed on his behalf.

“Eli.”

“What?”

“Calm down.”

“I am calm.”

“You literally yelled ‘no way.’”

Sara almost smiled for real.

The second guy held out a hand.

“Sorry. I’m Benji.”

“Sara.”

“I know.”

He winced.

“That sounded weird.”

“It did.”

Zac laughed.

Benji looked at him.

“And you’re—”

“Zac.”

“Yeah. Theo told us you guys were coming.”

Sara looked between them.

“Theo’s your brother?”

“Unfortunately,” Benji said.

Eli ignored him.

“I watched your last fight.”

Sara’s smile tightened just a little.

“Cool.”

“That finish was insane.”

“Thanks.”

“You thought she was gonna—”

Zac cut in before he could finish.

“She just won pretty recently.”

Eli looked at him.

Zac nodded toward Sara.

“She’s trying to forget work exists for a couple days.”

There was no edge in it.

No warning.

No speech about boundaries.

Just information.

Benji caught it immediately.

“Oh.”

He looked at Sara.

“Sorry.”

“You’re fine.”

“For real.”

“You’re fine.”

Eli looked disappointed for about half a second.

Then recovered completely.

“Theo said the gaming setup on the boat is crazy.”

Sara blinked.

That was fast.

Benji laughed.

“See? He’s over you.”

“I’m not over her. I’m just respecting boundaries.”

“You don’t know what boundaries are.”

“I know gaming.”

“That part is true.”

Sara laughed.

This time she meant it.

Eli pointed toward the Meridian.

“He said they upgraded the whole room. New PCs, huge monitors, some kind of time system on the access cards.”

Benji shook his head.

“He’s been talking about it all week.”

“Because it matters.”

“You’re gonna spend the whole trip inside.”

“That’s what air conditioning is for.”

Sara looked toward Zac.

He gave her a small smile.

Not the kind that asked if she was okay.

Not the kind that wanted credit.

Just a smile.

She noticed that.

And then hated that she noticed it.

They kept walking together.

Eli and Benji had already stopped treating her like somebody they’d seen on a screen and started arguing about who was going to beat who at whatever game Theo had promised them.

That was better.

Normal was better.

Sara shifted her bag higher on her shoulder.

Zac looked over.

“You want me to carry that?”

“No.”

“Okay.”

And that was it.

No second offer.

No reaching for it anyway.

No:

Are you sure?

Sara glanced at him.

He was looking ahead.

The boat got larger as they approached.

White hull.

Dark windows.

Two levels.

Open upper deck.

A narrow walkway along the side.

Clean enough that Sara immediately knew somebody had probably been yelled at about it that morning.

Benji pointed.

“That has to be Theo.”

A guy on the upper deck was leaning over the railing, waving both arms like they might somehow miss the boat.

“Yo!”

Eli waved back.

“There he is.”

Theo yelled something they couldn’t make out.

Benji yelled back:

“What?”

Theo repeated it louder.

Still impossible to understand.

Zac looked at Sara.

“Family reunion.”

“Apparently.”

She looked at the Meridian again.

It was quiet.

Private.

Small group.

Water in every direction once they left.

For the first time that morning, she thought maybe this could actually work.

Maybe she could disappear for a little while without going anywhere alone.

Then somebody on the boat called her name.

Sara closed her eyes.

Zac laughed beside her.

“Almost made it.”

She elbowed him lightly in the arm.

“Don’t start.”

And together they headed toward the gangway.
