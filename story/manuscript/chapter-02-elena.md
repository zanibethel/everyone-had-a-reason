# Chapter 2
## Elena

Elena hated fitted sheets.

Not because they were difficult.

Because everyone acted like they weren’t.

She hooked one corner over the mattress, pulled the opposite side tight, and watched the first corner snap free.

“Of course.”

She stared at it for a second.

Then bent down and fixed it again.

The cabin was one of Meridian’s nicer ones, though Adrian would probably have called that an insult.

According to him, they were all nice.

This one just had better lighting, a wider window, and enough decorative pillows to make actually sleeping feel like an administrative failure.

Elena tossed the final pillow into place.

Crooked.

She adjusted it.

Still crooked.

She adjusted it again.

The door opened behind her.

“You know nobody actually sleeps with all those.”

She didn’t turn around.

“You paying me to make beds or critique pillows?”

“Depends. Which one costs more?”

Now she smiled.

She knew his voice too well.

Adrian stepped inside and closed the door behind him.

Elena kept smoothing the duvet as if that mattered.

“How are your boys upstairs?”

“My boys?”

“The ones you were just yelling at.”

“I wasn’t yelling.”

“You have a voice you use when you want people to know you’re disappointed in them.”

“I have no idea what you’re talking about.”

She turned.

“You absolutely do.”

Adrian leaned against the door.

“Niko and Theo are dragging their feet.”

“They’re working.”

“They’re talking.”

“People can do both.”

“Not efficiently.”

Elena shook her head.

“There it is.”

“What?”

“That thing you do.”

“What thing?”

“Where everything has to happen exactly when you decide it should.”

Adrian smiled.

“You like that about me.”

“Sometimes.”

He crossed the room.

Elena should have stepped away.

Instead she let him come closer.

His hand settled lightly at her waist.

She looked toward the closed door.

“Seriously?”

“What?”

“You just came down here after getting onto my husband.”

“He’ll survive.”

“That’s not what I meant.”

Adrian kissed her.

Whatever argument she had been about to make disappeared.

She kissed him back.

For a few seconds, the cabin stopped feeling like another room she had to clean before somebody arrived.

Adrian pulled away first.

Elena looked toward the door again.

“Careful.”

He smiled.

“My husband will see us.”

“Niko?”

“No, the other husband I keep on board.”

Adrian laughed.

“He’s upstairs.”

“You sure?”

“I just left him there.”

“With Theo?”

“Still finding new ways to make cleaning a deck take an hour.”

Elena smiled despite herself.

“So we have time?”

Adrian stepped closer again.

“We have time.”

“You’re terrible.”

“You say that like you’re leaving.”

“I should.”

“But?”

She looked at him.

“But apparently I make bad decisions.”

“That one hurt.”

“You’ll recover.”

He kissed her again.

This time she laughed into it.

Then Adrian’s phone rang.

He stopped.

Elena closed her eyes.

“Perfect.”

Adrian checked the screen.

“Zac.”

“Already?”

“Looks like it.”

He answered.

“Hey.”

Elena went back to the bed while he listened.

She could hear the distant sound of Zac’s voice but not the words.

Adrian glanced through the cabin window toward the dock.

“Yeah, I see you. Give me a minute. I’ll meet you up front.”

He hung up.

“Showtime?” Elena asked.

“Apparently.”

Adrian slipped the phone into his pocket.

For half a second he stayed where he was.

Then the change happened.

It was subtle.

Shoulders back.

Smile easier.

Attention outward.

Captain Adrian.

Host Adrian.

The man who owned the boat and wanted everyone aboard it to know they were lucky to be there.

Elena watched him.

“You’re doing it.”

“Doing what?”

“The face.”

“What face?”

“The one where suddenly you love everybody.”

“I do love everybody.”

“You were just threatening Theo over towels.”

“I can contain multitudes.”

She snorted.

Adrian reached for the door.

Before he opened it, Elena said:

“Hey.”

He looked back.

For a moment she almost said something else.

Something heavier.

Instead:

“Try not to scare them off before we leave the dock.”

Adrian smiled.

“No promises.”

Then he was gone.

Elena stood alone in the cabin.

The room went quiet again.

She looked at the bed.

One pillow was crooked.

She fixed it.

Then stared at it longer than necessary.

Outside, voices were starting to carry from the dock.

Guests arriving.

Theo laughing somewhere above.

Adrian already welcoming somebody like he hadn’t been kissing her less than a minute earlier.

Elena reached for the laundry basket.

Her charm bracelet slid down her wrist with the movement, the small pieces clicking softly against one another.

A silver heart.

A tiny pair of dice.

A miniature key.

And the little mirrored disco ball Niko had bought her years ago because she’d made him dance with her in a bar he swore he hated.

She turned her wrist once.

Watched the tiny mirrors catch the light.

Then picked up the basket and headed for the next room.
