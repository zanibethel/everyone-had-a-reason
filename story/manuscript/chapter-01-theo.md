# Chapter 1
## Theo

Theo had learned there were two versions of the Meridian.

There was the one people paid to see: polished teak, spotless glass, folded towels, quiet water, and enough chrome to make every surface look expensive.

Then there was the version that existed before anybody arrived.

That version had extension cords across the deck, half-open storage bins, spray bottles sitting where drinks would be in an hour, and Niko leaning over a table like he was considering whether throwing the entire thing overboard would save time.

“You missed a spot,” Theo said.

Niko looked up.

“Come clean it.”

Theo grinned and kept tightening the strap on one of the deck loungers.

The morning sun had barely burned through the haze over the marina, but the deck was already warm. Meridian sat low and still against the dock, tied off beneath a row of white poles that knocked gently against one another whenever the water moved.

In a few hours, none of this would look like work.

That was the point.

Theo stepped back from the lounger.

“Benji’s gonna love this place.”

Niko glanced toward him. “Which one is Benji again?”

“The social one.”

“That helps.”

“The one who knows everybody.”

“Oh. The one who says yes to everything.”

Theo laughed. “Pretty much.”

He picked up a stack of towels and started arranging them on the shelf beside the fitness entrance.

“If you need anything this weekend, ask Benji. Dude would give you his last twenty bucks and apologize that it wasn’t forty.”

Niko smiled despite himself.

“That sounds exhausting.”

“It is. Especially when you’re his brother.”

Theo folded another towel.

“And Eli’s the opposite. If he finds that gaming room, we’re not seeing him again.”

“The new one?”

“Yeah.”

Niko looked toward the lower deck.

“Adrian spent enough on it.”

“Theo said—” Niko stopped himself and smirked. “You said those computers are ridiculous.”

“They are.”

“So Eli’s done.”

“Absolutely.”

Theo tossed the last towel onto the stack.

“He’ll say he’s getting off after one more game until we dock.”

Niko shook his head.

“Healthy family.”

“Very.”

Theo leaned against the counter for half a second before remembering Adrian would probably materialize if he saw him standing still.

He went back to work.

“You excited?”

“For what?”

“The trip.”

Niko gave him a look.

“I work here.”

“That doesn’t mean you can’t enjoy it.”

“Tell Adrian.”

Theo laughed again, but Niko didn’t.

Something had been off with him all morning.

Not enough for Theo to ask at first. Niko always got irritated before guests arrived. Kitchen prep, inventory, people touching things they weren’t supposed to touch—it was almost part of the routine.

But this felt different.

Theo grabbed another chair.

“Elena coming up later?”

Niko didn’t answer immediately.

That got Theo’s attention.

He looked over.

Niko was wiping down the same section of table he had already cleaned.

“What?”

Niko shrugged.

“Nothing.”

“That wasn’t nothing.”

“It’s fine.”

Theo waited.

Niko kept wiping.

Then finally:

“Things have just been weird.”

Theo straightened.

“With Elena?”

Niko nodded once.

That surprised him.

“Really?”

Niko looked at him.

“What?”

“I thought y’all were doing good.”

A short laugh came out of Niko, but there wasn’t much humor in it.

“Yeah. Everybody does.”

Theo set the chair down.

“What happened?”

“I don’t know.”

“You don’t know?”

“That’s kind of the problem.”

Niko dropped the rag onto the table.

“She’s just… somewhere else lately.”

Theo watched him.

“Like mad at you?”

“I almost wish.”

That answer landed harder than Theo expected.

Niko crossed his arms.

“She’s not really arguing. She just doesn’t talk. Everything’s later.”

“Later?”

“Can we talk later. I’ll do it later. We’ll figure it out later.”

Theo nodded slowly.

“You ask her what’s going on?”

Niko looked away.

Theo waited.

“You didn’t.”

“I have.”

“No, you asked her if she was mad. That’s different.”

Niko gave him a look.

“Now you’re a therapist?”

“No. I have brothers. Same thing.”

“That explains a lot.”

Theo smiled.

Niko didn’t.

He looked toward the railing instead.

For a few seconds there was only the sound of water against the hull and a gull somewhere beyond the marina.

“We used to talk about leaving,” Niko said.

Theo frowned. “Leaving what?”

“Here.”

“The Meridian?”

“Yeah.”

“Together?”

Niko nodded.

“Eventually. Doing our own thing.”

“What kind of thing?”

“I don’t know. Restaurant. Catering. Something where I’m not cooking for people who think asking for ranch counts as a personality.”

Theo laughed.

“That sounds like a very specific person.”

“You’ll meet them eventually.”

“But seriously, that sounds good.”

“It was just talk.”

“You don’t know that.”

Niko picked the rag back up.

“That’s the thing. I don’t know anything right now.”

Theo was about to say something when a voice came from behind them.

“You know what I know?”

They both turned.

Adrian stood near the stairwell.

He was wearing dark slacks and a light button-down with the sleeves rolled neatly to his forearms. Even before the guests arrived, Adrian somehow always looked like somebody might take his picture.

He looked around the deck.

Then at Theo.

Then Niko.

“Guests are gonna be here soon.”

“We’re almost done,” Theo said.

Adrian’s eyes moved to the stack of towels.

One was slightly crooked.

He fixed it.

Then he pointed toward the far table.

“Glassware?”

“Coming.”

“The center section?”

“Done.”

Adrian walked over to it.

He adjusted one chair.

Maybe an inch.

Theo noticed Niko notice.

Neither said anything.

Adrian looked toward the fitness entrance.

“Still equipment out in there?”

“I’m setting it up.”

“Then set it up.”

“I am.”

Adrian looked at him.

Theo immediately regretted answering.

“Almost done doesn’t mean done.”

Niko muttered something under his breath.

Adrian turned.

“What was that?”

Niko looked up.

“Nothing.”

“Then move.”

Niko’s jaw tightened.

Theo grabbed the spray bottle before either of them made the situation worse.

“We got it.”

Adrian looked at the two of them for another moment.

Then nodded.

“Good.”

He started toward the stairs.

Theo waited until Adrian was halfway down.

Niko said quietly:

“He acts like this whole boat stops spinning if something’s two inches out of place.”

Theo looked toward the stairwell.

Then back at Niko.

“You should probably fix those chairs.”

Niko stared at him.

Theo smiled.

“Man, shut up.”

Theo laughed and went back to work.

Behind them, the Meridian shifted gently against the dock.

Neither of them paid any attention to it.
