# Chapter 6
## Zak

Zak knew something was wrong before his friend finished the first sentence.

He stepped farther from the cockpit door and lowered his voice.

“What do you mean you’re not coming?”

A pause.

Zak looked back through the glass.

Adrian was still inside, staring at the route display like nothing had changed.

“The trip, or the deal?”

Another pause.

Zak closed his eyes.

“Both?”

He rubbed a hand over his face.

“No, man. You can’t do that.”

The answer came fast enough to irritate him more.

“I know you can back out. That’s not what I’m saying.”

He paced a few steps down the corridor.

“You’ve had weeks to figure this out.”

His friend said something about timing.

Risk.

Numbers.

Zak stopped.

“Then you should’ve told him.”

Another excuse.

Zak looked toward the cockpit again.

Adrian had taken on debt believing this deal was coming.

Zak had vouched for both sides.

That was the part bothering him most.

Not that the investor changed his mind.

People changed their minds.

Money moved.

Deals died.

That happened.

But now Zak was standing on Adrian’s boat while the person who was supposed to deliver the bad news sat comfortably somewhere else.

“Man, I can’t be here knowing all that.”

His friend asked him not to make it dramatic.

Zak laughed once.

“No, what would’ve been less dramatic is you showing up and telling him yourself.”

Someone moved behind him.

Zak turned.

Elena was standing a few feet away with a basket of folded towels against one hip.

He couldn’t tell how long she had been there.

Her expression answered the question.

Long enough.

Zak looked away.

“Gotta go.”

He ended the call.

Elena stared at him.

“He’s not coming?”

Zak slid his phone into his pocket.

“It’s complicated.”

“That sounded pretty clear.”

Zak exhaled.

“Elena.”

“He backed out?”

Zak didn’t answer.

She shifted the towels higher.

“Zak.”

“Yeah.”

Her face changed.

“The whole thing?”

“Yeah.”

She looked toward the cockpit.

Zak stepped slightly into her line of sight.

“Don’t.”

Her eyes came back to him.

“Don’t what?”

“Go in there.”

“Why?”

“Because I’m working on it.”

Elena looked at him like he’d said something ridiculous.

“Working on what?”

“Finding somebody else.”

“Right now?”

“Yes.”

“Zak, this isn’t a dinner reservation.”

“I know what it is.”

“Do you?”

He looked at her.

That annoyed him more than it should have.

Of course he knew.

He had brought Adrian into the conversation in the first place.

Adrian had a vessel he wanted to expand.

Zak had people with money.

That was what Zak did.

Connections.

Introductions.

Put the right person in front of the right opportunity and take a piece if it closed.

Simple.

Except it was never simple once people were involved.

Niko and Elena were friends.

Adrian was becoming a client.

The investor was Zak’s connection.

If the deal worked, everybody won.

If it died now, Zak was the person standing in the middle of all of them.

“I can fix it,” he said.

Elena’s eyebrows lifted.

“You can replace an investor in an hour?”

“I can find somebody interested.”

“That’s not the same thing.”

“I know.”

“Then Adrian needs to know.”

Zak shook his head.

“Not yet.”

Her expression hardened.

“He just showed you how much money he put into this.”

“You heard that?”

“I know how much he’s put into this.”

That made Zak pause.

Elena knew more about the business than he had assumed.

“He thinks that money is coming,” she said.

“I know.”

“So tell him.”

“I will.”

“When?”

“When I have something else to give him besides ‘everything just fell apart.’”

Elena stared at him.

Zak softened his voice.

“Give me a little time.”

She looked toward the cockpit again.

Zak could see the decision forming.

“No.”

“Elena.”

“He deserves to know.”

“If you tell him now, you’re going to make everything worse.”

The words came out harder than he intended.

Elena went still.

Zak immediately regretted the tone.

“That’s not what I meant.”

“It sounded pretty clear.”

“I’m saying give me time to fix it.”

“You keep saying fix it like this is yours.”

Zak didn’t have an answer for that.

She shifted the basket again.

“I’m not promising anything.”

Then she walked away.

Zak watched her disappear down the corridor.

He pulled his phone back out.

Opened their message thread.

They all had each other’s numbers. Dinners, trips, work favors, group plans. Niko sending pictures of food. Elena asking whether Zak was bringing somebody to an event. Normal stuff.

Zak typed:

**Please don’t tell him yet. I just need a little time.**

He stared at it.

Added:

**I’m trying to find someone else.**

Still not enough.

Then:

**If you tell him now, you’re going to make everything worse. Please just give me a chance to fix it first.**

He hit send.

The message showed delivered.

Unread.

Zak immediately opened his contacts.

First call.

No answer.

Second.

Voicemail.

Third picked up.

Zak smiled automatically.

“Hey, man. I’ve got something I think you might be interested in.”

Three minutes later, he was listening to another excuse.

He ended the call.

Scrolled again.

People he had introduced to people.

People he had vouched for.

People who had called him when they needed something moved, sold, funded, booked, fixed.

Zak selected another name.

Paused.

Then hit call.

Somewhere down the corridor, Elena’s phone remained unanswered.
