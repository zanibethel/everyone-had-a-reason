# Chapter 5
## Zak

Zak had expected a nice boat.

He had not expected a cockpit that looked like somebody had crossed a private jet with a gaming rig.

Three wide displays curved around the captain’s station. A smaller screen sat off to the side with weather, route, depth, and what looked like half a dozen other things Zak didn’t understand. Most of the physical controls were surprisingly simple.

Adrian noticed him looking.

“Cleaner than you expected?”

“Way cleaner.”

“That was the point.”

Adrian stepped behind the main station and tapped the center display.

A bright line traced out from the marina and curved offshore before looping back toward land.

Zak leaned closer.

“That’s the route?”

“Yep.”

“How long?”

“Few hours out, few hours back. We’re on a timed loop.”

Zak looked toward the windows.

“So you’re just driving this thing the whole time?”

Adrian smiled.

“No.”

Zak looked at him.

“No?”

“That’s what’s cool about the upgrade.”

He touched something on the display.

A green indicator appeared.

**AUTO NAVIGATION ACTIVE**

The boat was still tied to the dock, but the route line brightened.

Adrian pointed to it.

“Once we’re clear, this handles most of the work.”

“Like autopilot?”

“More than that.”

He started tapping through screens.

“It holds course, corrects for current, adjusts for wind, tracks obstacles, avoids traffic, then brings us back toward the route.”

Zak looked at him.

“So who drives?”

“Nobody, most of the time.”

“That feels unsafe.”

Adrian laughed.

“It’s safer than people.”

“Great sales pitch.”

“I’m serious.”

He pointed toward another section of the display.

“Radar, cameras, proximity sensors. If something gets in the way, it adjusts. If the current pushes us, it corrects.”

“And if you need to take over?”

Adrian pressed a control.

The green indicator disappeared.

A warning replaced it.

**MANUAL CONTROL  
COLLISION AVOIDANCE LIMITED**

Zak read it.

“That seems less reassuring.”

“That’s because manual means manual.”

Adrian rested one hand on the controls.

“Normally, I leave this on.”

He reactivated the system.

The warning disappeared.

**AUTO NAVIGATION ACTIVE**

“You could practically drive it yourself.”

Zak laughed.

“Absolutely not.”

“I’m telling you, if you can follow a screen, you’d be fine.”

“Until I put us into another boat.”

“It won’t let you if this is on.”

“Okay, that part I like.”

Adrian grinned.

“This whole trip used to require somebody sitting up here almost the entire time. Now I can actually be with everybody.”

Zak looked around.

“So you’ll just be hanging out with us?”

“That’s the idea.”

“And this thing takes us back?”

“Exactly. Route’s already loaded. We leave, run the loop, come back in later.”

Zak nodded slowly.

That was impressive.

More impressive than he wanted to admit.

The Meridian shifted beneath them.

Not much.

Just enough that Zak caught himself against the console.

A drawer beside Adrian’s station slid open.

Zak glanced down.

For a second, he saw black metal inside.

A handgun.

Adrian pushed the drawer closed with his leg.

No comment.

No explanation.

He kept talking.

“Those little movements are normal.”

Zak looked back at him.

“What?”

“The correction.”

Adrian pointed toward the display.

“Wind, current, wake from another vessel. Once we’re moving, you’ll feel it sometimes.”

Zak glanced once at the closed drawer.

Then let it go.

Captain had a gun.

Not exactly the strangest thing he had ever seen.

Adrian moved to another screen.

“This is where it gets expensive.”

Zak looked over.

“Finally.”

Adrian laughed.

“Navigation upgrade, credential system, room controls, gaming, security, all of it talks to the same backbone now.”

“Sounds cheap.”

“Very.”

“How cheap?”

Adrian looked at him.

“Cheap enough that I don’t like saying the number out loud.”

Zak smiled.

Then noticed Adrian wasn’t really joking.

“You financed all this?”

“Most of it.”

“Before the investment?”

Adrian shrugged.

“Had to.”

Zak frowned.

“You had to?”

“If I waited until everything was guaranteed, I’d still be running the old setup.”

He tapped the display.

“This is the proof.”

“Proof of what?”

“That this works.”

Adrian’s energy changed when he talked about it.

Not louder.

Sharper.

Like he could already see something that didn’t exist yet.

“One Meridian is nice.”

He gestured around them.

“Two is a business.”

Another tap.

“Five is a brand.”

Zak looked at him.

“You already planning five?”

“I’m planning more than five.”

Zak laughed.

“You don’t think small.”

“That’s how people stay small.”

He leaned back against the console.

“Your friend sees this in person, sees how automated it is, sees the margins…”

Zak’s stomach tightened slightly.

Adrian kept going.

“This boat was the expensive part. The next one gets easier. We replicate it.”

Zak looked through the front glass toward the marina.

“What if he doesn’t like it?”

Adrian smiled.

“He will.”

“You sound pretty sure.”

“I am.”

That answer bothered Zak more than it should have.

Not because Adrian was arrogant.

Zak knew plenty of arrogant people.

It was the confidence.

Like the investment had already happened.

Like the future had already agreed to cooperate.

Adrian checked his phone.

Nothing.

His expression shifted.

Just slightly.

Zak noticed.

“He say anything?”

“No.”

“He’s probably still on the way.”

Adrian nodded.

“Yeah.”

But he looked toward the marina again.

Zak pulled out his phone.

“I’ll call him.”

Adrian looked relieved.

“Do that.”

Zak stepped away from the controls.

He glanced once more at the route glowing across the screen.

Out.

Around.

Back to shore.

Simple.

Then he walked toward the cockpit door and hit call.
